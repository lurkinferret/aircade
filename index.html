    Whitepaper

    Dook Dash features

    Dook Dash is a one-thumb endless runner. A ferret runs down a three-lane track, dodging barriers, grabbing coins and boosts, and staying ahead of a hawk. Each run takes place in one of four environments. Coins collected in a run are saved and spent on upgrades that make boosts last longer, and on fur and hats for the ferret. Three daily goals give a reason to come back each day.

    This document describes only what the game itself does.

    Notes

    • One swipe makes exactly one move, however far the finger travels. Lift and swipe again for the next move.
    • Pressing down while in the air drops the ferret quickly and starts a slide the moment it lands.
    • A jump lasts about two thirds of a second. A slide lasts 0.6 seconds.
    • A sound on/off toggle sits in the corner of the screen.
    • Swiping toward a side wall when already in the outer lane is a stumble (see Edge bump).
    • Swipe left or right near a corner wall to take the corner (see Corner turns).
    • Menus use plain taps on buttons.

    2. THE RUN

    Track

    Three lanes. The ferret runs forward on its own; the player only steers, jumps and slides.

    Speed

    Starts at 15 and rises by 0.011 per meter, reaching the cap of 34 at about 1,730 m. A stumble slows the ferret to 60% speed for 0.7 seconds. War Dance adds 20% on top.

    The hawk

    A meter at the top of the screen shows how close the hawk is.

    • Each stumble (hitting a barrier) adds half a bar.
    • The bar drains slowly (2% per second) while the ferret runs clean.
    • A full bar ends the run.
    • As the bar fills, the hawk's shadow grows at the bottom of the screen and its wings flap.

    Scoring

    Score = distance in meters + 10 per coin + bonuses. Bonuses: 25 for picking up a sock, 5 for each barrier smashed during War Dance, 15 for a close call, 50 for a corner turned. The best score is saved.

    Edge bump

    The outer lanes have a wall beside them. One swipe too many toward that wall is a stumble (the hawk gets half a bar closer), and a shield absorbs it like any other hit. During War Dance and the short safety after a shield hit or a continue, the bump does nothing.

    Close calls

    Dodge a barrier at the last moment by leaving its lane when you are about to hit it, and you get +15 score and a CLOSE CALL banner. Jumping or sliding over a barrier in your own lane does not count, and neither does anything during War Dance.

    Corner turns

    About every 2,500 m (the first at roughly 2,500 m, then 2,200 to 2,800 m apart) the path ends in a hazard-striped wall with a big arrow showing which way it turns. Patterns stop appearing shortly before the wall. A flashing SWIPE LEFT / SWIPE RIGHT prompt shows when the wall is close.

    • Swipe in the arrow's direction before reaching the wall. The ferret turns, the screen swings, and you get +50. The ferret is put back in the middle lane.
    • A swipe the other way moves a lane as usual. If you do not swipe the right way in time, the ferret hits the wall: a stumble. The run goes on after the turn either way.
    • During War Dance the corner is taken automatically.

    Burrow bonus

    Taking a corner successfully drops the ferret into a burrow: about 300 m of earth tunnel with no barriers, only coins. The sky turns to a dark earth ceiling and the edges darken. Coins come as full rows across all three lanes, a winding single trail, or an arc you jump along. The hawk bar drains six times faster than usual (12% per second) while in the burrow, and normal patterns return when it ends. Missing the corner gives no burrow.

    Continue (once per run)

    When the hawk would end the run, a screen offers to keep going for coins: 100, plus 25 for each full 500 m run so far, up to 500. It is paid from saved coins first, then coins collected in this run. Only offered if you can afford it and have not continued yet. Continuing empties the hawk bar, clears barriers within the next 30 m and gives 3 seconds of safety. End run takes you to the game-over screen.

    3. BARRIERS

    Each barrier has one correct answer, or two for the bar.

    Low wall
    Stacked logs that block the bottom opening. Jump over it. Sliding does not work.
    Bar
    A thin bar on posts with an opening below it. Slide under it OR jump over it.
    Tall gate
    A double-height gate with an opening below it, striped with hazard tape. Slide under it. It is too tall to jump.
    Crate
    A solid block. Change lane.
    Spiked roller
    A red spiked barrel that sweeps side to side across all three lanes. Jump over it.

    Hitting any barrier is a stumble. Barriers are also smashed outright during War Dance.

    4. TRACK PATTERNS

    The track is generated as the ferret runs, from nine patterns. Each one is placed with coins that reward the correct move. The gap between patterns shrinks as speed rises.

    Single barrier
    One barrier (low wall, bar, gate or crate) with a coin line in a free lane.
    Crate wall
    Two crates side by side with one open lane and coins.
    Wall of logs
    Low walls across all three lanes, with coins arcing over one of them. Everyone must jump.
    Overhead wall
    Gates (60%) or bars (40%) across all three lanes, with coins underneath. Everyone must slide, or jump if it is a bar.
    Jump then slide
    A row of low walls, then a row of gates right after.
    Weave
    Four crate walls in a row, each with one open lane. The open lane shifts by at most one lane each time.
    Rollers
    One sweeping roller. After 250 m, two rollers moving in opposite phase.
    Three-way
    A different barrier in each lane (low wall, gate or bar, crate), shuffled. After 400 m a second one follows directly after the first.
    Coin trail
    A winding trail of coins that dips over a low wall, rewarding a jump.

    Spawn weights: single 8%, crate wall 12%, logs 10%, overhead wall 8%, jump then slide 16%, weave 14%, rollers 12%, three-way 14%, coin trail 6%.

    5. COINS

    • Each coin is worth 1 coin and 10 score points.
    • Coins sit in lines, arcs and trails. Some are high (collect by jumping) and some sit low under bars and gates.
    • Coins collected in a run are added to a saved total when the run ends.

    6. BOOSTS

    Boost pickups float above the track. Only one boost is active at a time. Picking up the same boost again is ignored and does not reset its timer. Picking up a different boost replaces the current one immediately.

    Sock (War Dance)
    Invincible, 20% faster, and smashes through every barrier (5 bonus points each). Picking one up gives 25 bonus points. The ferret hops and glows with orbiting sparkles.
    Magnet
    Pulls coins from every lane toward the ferret and collects them at any height. A red ring pulses around the ferret.
    Shield
    A bubble that blocks hits. Each blocked hit uses up 12 seconds of the shield and gives 0.7 seconds of safety. The bubble flickers in its last two seconds.
    Gold Rush (x2)
    Every coin counts double.
    Turbo
    For 6 seconds (not upgradeable) the ferret runs 25% faster, turns see-through and slips through every barrier without a hit. Each barrier passed gives 3 bonus points and picking one up gives 20. No close-call bonus while it runs.

    How often boosts appear

    After the first 10 seconds, and then after a cooldown, each new track pattern has a 20% chance to include a boost. Cooldown is about 22 seconds. Mix: Sock 2/8, Magnet 2/8, Shield 2/8, Gold Rush 1/8, Turbo 1/8. The active boost shows as a colored chip with its remaining seconds at the top of the screen, and a banner announces each pickup.

    7. UPGRADES

    Open from the start screen or the game-over screen. Saved coins pay for upgrades. Each upgrade has a single button showing the next price; the button turns to MAX at the top level.

    Boost lengths (War Dance, Magnet, Shield, Gold Rush)

    Level 0: 10 seconds. Each level adds 5 seconds. Level 22 (max) is 2 minutes. Every boost upgrades separately.

    Boost Finder

    Makes boosts appear more often. Each level shortens the cooldown by one second (22 s down to 8 s at level 14, the max) and adds 3% to the chance that a pattern includes a boost.

    8. WORLDS

    Four environments. Each run uses one, chosen at random and never the same as the previous run. The first run uses the world shown on the start screen. The world's name appears as a banner when the run starts and beside the distance in the top bar. The world does not change during a run.

    Garden
    Dusk sky with stars and hills. Trees and bushes beside the path.
    Sewer
    Brick ceiling with pipes and drips, a green light at the tunnel end, murky water, and glowing lamp-pipes beside the path.
    Attic
    Wooden rafters, a shaft of window light, drifting dust, a plank floor, and stacked cardboard boxes beside the path. Has the lights-out lane (below).
    Rooftops
    Night sky with a moon and stars, a lit city skyline, a tar path with parapet walls, brick chimneys and a blinking antenna.
    Kitchen
    Tiled wall with swinging hanging pots, a sunny window, steam, a cream tile floor, and stoves and jars beside the path.
    Beach
    Sunset sky with a sun on the sea, drifting clouds, palms on the horizon, a sand path, and palm trees, striped umbrellas and shells beside the path.

    Each world has its own sky, ground, path, walls, fog and roadside scenery. Barriers and boosts behave the same in every world.

    Attic twist: lights out on one lane

    Only in the Attic. The first lights-out comes 18 seconds into the run, then one every 14 to 22 seconds.

    • One lane is chosen at random. It flickers for 1 second as a warning.
    • Then it goes dark for 4 seconds. The lane is covered by a dark shade.
    • The light comes back over the next 0.3 seconds.
    • Anything in the dark lane (barriers, coins, boosts) is faint while far away: fully visible within 4 m, fading to a faint shape at 10 m and beyond. Things in the other two lanes look normal.
    • Nothing about the rules changes. The dark lane works exactly like a lit one; it is only harder to read.

    9. DAILY GOALS

    Three goals per day. They are the same all day and change at local midnight. Each goal has a target and a coin reward. Progress adds up across every run that day and is counted when a run ends.

    Goal types (three different types are picked each day)

    Collect coins
    Coins grabbed. Targets 60 / 100 / 150.
    Run in total
    Meters run, all runs added. Targets 800 / 1,500 / 2,500.
    Slide under
    Gates or bars cleared by sliding. Targets 8 / 15 / 25.
    Jump over
    Barriers cleared by jumping (low walls, rollers, bars). Targets 8 / 15 / 25.
    Pick up boosts
    Boost pickups. Targets 3 / 5 / 8.
    Single run
    Reach a distance in one run (best run counts). Targets 400 / 700 / 1,000 m.
    Smash barriers
    Barriers smashed during War Dance. Targets 8 / 15 / 25.

    Each goal is easy, medium or hard (the target is the first, second or third number above). Rewards: 25, 40 or 60 coins. Rewards are paid when the run ends and shown on the game-over screen.

    Finish all three

    Completing all three goals in a day pays a bonus of 50 coins, plus 10 for each day of streak beyond the first, up to 100.

    Streak

    The streak counts consecutive days with at least one finished run. Missing a day resets it. The goals screen shows the current streak.

    Where it appears

    The Goals button on the start and game-over screens shows how many are done (for example "Goals 1/3"). The goals screen lists each goal with a progress bar.

    Whitepaper · Ferret Arcade

    Designing small games around a ferret's habits

    Ferrets sleep most of the day and spend the rest in short, intense bursts. This paper describes how the six games in this arcade borrow from those bursts, which numbers control their difficulty, how one shared coin wallet and a layer of cosmetics, badges and titles tie them together, and how the larger runner, Dook Dash, fits alongside them.

    1. Why ferrets suit arcade rounds

    Pet ferrets commonly sleep 14 to 18 hours a day. When they wake, they sprint, tunnel through anything tube-shaped, carry off small objects such as socks and raisins, pile into hammocks, and make a chuckling sound called dooking when they are excited. Each of these happens in bursts of a minute or two, which is the same length as a good arcade round. We used that as the design brief: every game should be understood in one sentence and finished in a few minutes. Each game also has its own field guide on its page, where every feature can be opened and many can be tried with one tap.

    2. Six games, six behaviors

    GameBehavior borrowedCore loopNumbers that set difficulty
    Sock MatchStashing small objectsFlip two cards, remember where each sock wasFree play at 6, 8 or 10 pairs, levels that grow to 10 pairs, and a daily board seeded from the date. Three stars at 1.6 moves per pair or fewer, two at 2.6, with each decoy on the board adding its cost to both limits. Decoys cost 2 moves, a golden pair gives 2 back, and from level 2 a mischief ferret swaps two face-down cards every few misses. Reveal and Freeze cost 15 and 5 coins.
    Tunnel DashTunnelingSwitch between 3 lanes, jump shoes and gaps, duck pipes, dodge vacuums, collectSpeed rises from 170 to 380 px/s over the first 60 s, then keeps climbing to 450 px/s while obstacles arrive faster and more often in pairs. 3 lives, up to 5. The tunnel changes every 400 m, cycling through four biomes (Laundry room, Garden hose, Couch fort and the Craft closet, whose rolling yarn balls must be jumped), with a checkpoint every 500 m. From 600 m, and every 700 m after, a giant vacuum starts a boss chase of 7 flagged-lane attacks, growing to 12. Each warning lasts 1.2 s and shortens with every attack and every chase, to a floor of 0.6 s. Every second boss is a cat paw instead: it swats one lane and then a neighbouring lane half a second later, in about 30 percent fewer attacks. Three missions at a time pay 10 to 30 coins each.

    3. Difficulty without punishment

    Every game restarts with one tap, and the action games ramp up slowly. In Ferret Gems, a swap that makes no match slides back and costs no move, and the Hint button is free. In Tunnel Dash, no more than two of the three lanes are ever blocked in the same row, so a clear lane always exists, a boss chase flags at most two lanes at a time, a shield absorbs one hit, and a hit gives 1.2 seconds of protection so one mistake does not chain into three. In Pop-Up Pals, most pop-ups are ordinary ferrets, with golden ferrets, clocks and vacuums mixed in to reward attention. Sock Match shows a clock but ranks you by moves only, so players can take their time. Hide the Raisin always shows the raisin first and gives the player a peek, and for players who find it too easy it has Twin socks, where every sock is the same colour so only position can be followed, with 1 extra sock at each stage (up to 6) and scores multiplied by 1.5. Hammock Stack forgives small misses by trimming the hammock rather than ending the game.

    4. One wallet, many places to spend it

    All six games pay into the same coin wallet, and it is spent in four places: Gem boosters, Sock Match power-ups, Hide the Raisin peeks, and the Ferret shop. The shop sells cosmetics, which never change scoring: 12 hats (40 to 150 coins), 6 coats (60 to 120), gem sets (70 to 90), board themes (50 to 80), sock card backs (50 to 90), and backgrounds (50 to 70) and frames (50 to 90) for the pictures made by Copy my result. It also sells boost packs for 15 to 90 coins. Packs are small conveniences, such as a starting shield, an extra heart, or a peek, and they are used up before any coins are spent. The aim is for coins to feel like a thank-you for playing any game, not a reason to grind one.

    5. Identity and long-term goals

    The Collection page lets a player try on any item, even ones they do not own, before buying. It also holds a ferret name of up to 16 characters and 17 titles, such as Sock Bandit or Frost Walker, which unlock from play stats. The name and title appear under the ferret on shared result pictures. There are 65 badges across the arcade, each paying 10 to 150 coins once. Dook Dash counts here too: its runs, distance, corners, close calls and daily goals feed badges, two titles and the quests, although its coins stay in its own wallet. Three daily quests, picked from different games by a generator seeded from the date, add a reason to try a game you have not played lately: each pays 15 to 35 coins, and finishing all three adds a bonus and a streak. Badges and titles are computed from the same stats that fill the My stats page, so nothing is tracked twice.

    The My ferret page (the ferret button in the header) pulls this identity together on one screen: the ferret in its current outfit, its name and title, 65 badges and 17 titles with the ones closest to unlocking, the daily streaks for quests, Sock Match, Pop-Up Pals, Hide the Raisin and Dook Dash goals, a weekly recap of games and coins that you can copy as a picture, three weekly goals (play on 5 days, play 20 games, earn 100 coins, counted Monday to Sunday) that pay 20 coins each plus 30 more for all three, a bonus goal of 5 daily medals in the week worth 25 coins, a look back at last week, streak rewards of 40 coins for a 7-day, 80 for a 14-day and 150 for a 30-day daily quest streak (each claimed once per streak), an all-time view of your best weeks, and a copyable ferret card. Streaks only count while they are alive, meaning played today or yesterday. Each daily can also earn a medal, kept per day and counted on the page: Sock Match gives gold, silver or bronze for three, two or one stars, Pop-Up Pals for 250, 150 or 80 points, and Hide the Raisin for 10, 8 or 5 raisins found, and the Ferret Gems daily puzzle for finishing with 30 or 15 percent of the moves left, or any clear for bronze.

    Progress lives in the browser, so the arcade gives players some control over it. The Settings page (the gear in the header) sets sound and volume, reduced motion, text size shape markers for the gems and letter labels for sock cards. It also makes a backup code: a block of text, with a check value to catch copying errors, that carries all progress, Dook Dash included. Pasting a code on another device shows a summary first. The player can then merge it, keeping the higher value of each item, with items and badges from both sides kept and coins not added together, or replace everything outright. Nothing changes until one is chosen. The code is not encrypted, so anyone holding it can load the progress. A Share today block on the quests page turns the day’s quests and daily boards into a few lines of text or a picture, with no server involved, so players can compare days with friends.

    6. Dook Dash, the larger runner

    Dook Dash is the arcade’s one-thumb endless runner, and the biggest game in it. A ferret runs down a three-lane track on its own while you swipe to change lane, jump or slide (arrow keys, WASD and Space also work). Hitting a barrier is a stumble, and each stumble brings a hawk half a bar closer. A full bar ends the run. The bar drains slowly (2% a second) while you run clean. It borrows the tunneling and bolting side of ferret play, and it has its own in-game whitepaper with every number.

    Speed starts at 15 and rises 0.011 per metre until it caps at 34, around 1,730 m. From there it keeps creeping up by 0.0026 per metre to a final top speed of 40, reached around 4,035 m. Score is distance, plus 10 per coin, plus bonuses: 25 for a sock, 5 per barrier smashed, 15 for a close call and 50 for a corner turned. Every barrier has a right answer, and the track is built from nine patterns whose gaps shrink as you speed up.

    Two features break up the long middle of a run. About every 2,500 m the path ends in a corner wall with an arrow: swipe that way in time and you turn the corner for 50 points and drop into a burrow, about 300 m of barrier-free earth tunnel full of coins where the hawk bar drains six times faster. Miss it and you stumble. Once per run, when the hawk would end it, you can pay coins to continue: 100, plus 25 for every full 500 m, up to 500. It empties the hawk bar and gives three seconds of safety.

    Each run uses one of six worlds, never the same as the last: Garden, Sewer, Attic, Rooftops, Kitchen and Beach. They change the look and nothing else, except the Attic, where one lane at a time flickers and then goes dark for four seconds. The dark lane works normally and is only harder to read.

    Dook Dash has its own long-term loop, kept separate from the arcade’s. Coins from a run pay for five upgrades: the length of each of the four upgradeable boosts (10 seconds at level 0, 5 more per level, 2 minutes at level 22) and Boost Finder, which makes boosts appear more often. Every upgrade follows one price curve, from 20 coins for the first level to 625 for the last. Maxing one boost costs 5,735 coins. A wardrobe of furs and hats is cosmetic only and costs 3,200 coins in all. Three daily goals, picked from ten types and worth 25, 40 or 60 coins, add a 50-coin bonus for finishing all three and a streak of consecutive days.

    The two halves are connected in a few places and not in others. Dook Dash’s runs, distance, corners, close calls and goals feed the arcade’s quests, badges, titles and My stats page, and one backup code carries its progress too. Its coins, upgrades and wardrobe stay in its own wallet and do not mix with the arcade’s. The sound switch, volume, reduced-motion and text-size settings apply to it, although the big countdown and pop-ups drawn on its track stay at a fixed size. Like the other games, none of its numbers have been playtested with other people.

    7. A weekly rhythm, friends and a first run

    Weekly goals reset every Monday, and a themed week sits on top of them. Each Monday one game becomes the theme, cycling through the same seven in the same order for everyone, so a given week has the same theme on every device. Play 5 rounds, runs or games of the theme game between Monday and Sunday and you can claim 30 coins and a stamp for that game. The theme is extra: it does not count toward the three-goal bonus. Stamps feed two badges, Week themer (finish 3 themed weeks, 50 coins) and Full rotation (a stamp in all 7 games, 150 coins), and a title, Week Collector, for 4 stamps.

    Thirteen seasonal events run each year, back to back, so there is nearly always one on: Frost Fest (Dec 10 to Jan 2), Cozy Burrow (Jan 3 to 31), Sweet Tooth (Feb 1 to Mar 1), Puddle Jumpers (Mar 2 to 19), Bloom Days (Mar 20 to Apr 10), Spring Showers (Apr 11 to May 10), Garden Party (May 11 to Jun 9), Long Days (Jun 10 to 30), Sun Splash (Jul 1 to 22), Camp Out (Jul 23 to Aug 31), Back to Burrow (Sep 1 to Oct 9), Harvest Hop (Oct 10 to Nov 1) and Nutty November (Nov 2 to Dec 9). Each finished game, in any game, earns one event token. Tokens unlock coins and an exclusive hat and board theme at 8, 20, 35 and 50 tokens. Unclaimed rewards are lost when the event ends, and the same event returns the next year with a fresh token count. Event items are shown as locked in the collection until you earn them. Settings has a view-only preview of every event, with its dates, hat and board theme, that never changes your progress.

    Friends can compare without accounts. My ferret makes a compare code that starts with FAC1- and holds your name and nine totals: games, coins and days played over the last 7 days, weekly goals reached, badges, titles, items owned, week stamps and your longest running streak. A friend pastes it, the arcade checks its check value, and the two of you appear side by side with the higher number marked in each row. The code is read as plain text and nothing from it is saved. Codes made on different days cover different dates, and the page says so.

    Anything ready to claim is surfaced where players already are. Small pills at the top of Home count ready daily quests, rewards and badges, and a number on the ferret button counts weekly goals, the theme week and streak rewards. New players also get a three-step welcome tour on Home, which they can skip and replay from Settings. On touch screens buttons and links are at least 40 px tall, and Tunnel Dash’s play area never scrolls the page, so a swipe always jumps or ducks instead of moving the page.
